**Process note.** I read the brief, including both questions, before I fetched any page. So my answer to Q1 was written after I had seen Q2. I did not change it afterwards.

**Prior idea of the project.** None, beyond the URL (scat-kg-tools, DynamicalSystemsGroup) and the acronym that the Getting started page expands as "Stakeholder groups, Concerns, Affordances and Technologies". I came to the pages cold.

Second, the reference pages read like man-page style command references. **Q2. Did an image, phrase or metaphor make an instruction harder to follow or understand?** Yes, two places.

1. Page: facilitate-architectures. Quote: "much as a player in a jazz combo builds on what the others play." The instruction it sits beside is to run the elicitation stage. I took the image to mean: listen, then build on what was said, rather than follow a fixed script. That clashes with the same page's "Say these in this order. Each line is its own turn", and with "Say them as written or in your own words". The image made it unclear whether I should follow the scripted lines or improvise. I had to choose between the two. This is the hardest instruction I found.

2. Page: facilitate-scenarios. Quote: "Yes, and. Whatever someone offers is taken up and built on." The instruction is to write each contribution down as offered and ask a question that adds to it. I read "Yes, and" as a stance rule: never test or correct. The page then says to "keep those words and say them aloud", and the closing line "Yes, and is there anything you want kept in the room?" is a question with a spoken tag. I could not tell whether the tag is required wording or a stylistic device. Less serious than point 1.

Minor: planning's "writing the chart before the gig" and "before anyone counts in" were hard to parse, but the instruction underneath (write a plan, nothing to file) was clear. The Chatham House frame is referred to without definition; the page supplies the words to say, so I could follow it.
