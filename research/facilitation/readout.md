# Facilitation readout (WP3-D3)

The strategy in brief, then what WP4 needs the graph to be able to show, then a pointer for each stage for WP7.
Detail and citations: `strategy.md`, `sources.md`. Sources: `ant-rdf` at commit `a2ec48e`; "KOI & The Unicorn"
(Block Science blog); "Building the Loop" (DOI 10.1111/epic.70009).

## The strategy in brief

SCAT facilitates like improvised jazz. Two moves run through every stage.

- **"Yes, and".** Whatever is offered is written down as offered, then built on with a question that adds, not one
  that tests. Nothing offered is turned away or merged into someone else's line. This follows `ant-rdf`'s manner:
  one question at a time, nothing pre-sorted, the person revises in their own words.
- **"From this perspective".** Every contribution is placed in the stakeholder group it comes from, and worded that
  way. `ant-rdf` treats a reading as provenance, not fact, and lets contradictory readings stand together.

Nobody is argued into agreement. The facilitator names tension aloud ("these two want opposite things from the same
moment") and records both sides. Silence is recorded as "no account offered", not taken as assent. Sessions run under
the Chatham House rule, with groups and not individuals, inside the vault's cap on stakeholder groups: a standpoint
that does not fit is folded into a group's description, never dropped. Ranking, voting and reaching agreement are
out of scope.

The published sources give the stance but not a live-room method. `ant-rdf` holds disagreement analytically, and
the essays say little about it, so the room moves are our own design, shaped by `ant-rdf`.

Scenarios (behavioural: people doing things in time, technology in the background) and architectures (structural:
parts, wholes and the concerns they address, people set aside) are captured separately, in separate sessions or
clearly separate halves.

## What the graph must be able to show (for WP4)

Capabilities, not terms or mechanisms. The first two are the minimum.

1. The same concern held by different stakeholder groups, each group's account of it kept distinct.
2. Concerns in tension: two concerns, or two groups' stances, that cannot both be fully met, shown as tension and
   not resolved.
3. Groups that share one concern while differing on others (margins of potential conflict).
4. Several accounts of one thing side by side, with the differences left in place and none presented as the answer.
5. For any item, who holds it, and what other groups say about it or leave unsaid.
6. A gap recorded as a fact: what a group or design does not address, or where no account was offered.
7. Matters left open, failed or contested, kept with their reason and not made to disappear.
8. Several designs for one concern, and several concerns for one design.
9. A proposed design held distinct from the stories that motivated it.
10. Raw capture kept apart from its later reading, so what was said survives any interpretation.
11. The origin, time and status (offered, drafted by someone else, or confirmed by its owner) of each contribution, and the correction history when a
    group revises its account.
12. Agreement between groups recorded as an observation, not as a settled answer.
13. What a group agreed to share outward, and a trace when something is withdrawn.
14. Reliance on a single design or element, and arrangements long left unexamined, shown as questions to look at.
15. Material that is unfinished, shown as unfinished and not as a fault.

## Pointers for WP7

- **J4, planning.** Write the planning guide around six acts: choose sessions and keep scenarios and architectures
  apart; cluster people into stakeholder groups within the cap by standpoint; seat every group in every session;
  frame Chatham House aloud and on the page; note where groups may share a concern yet differ on others; and plan how raw notes are taken and kept apart from
  any later reading.
  Show "yes, and" as a planner who adds a requested topic instead of trading it off, and "from this perspective" as
  each group named in a sentence a member would accept as theirs. See `strategy.md`, J4.
- **J5, capturing.** Write two facilitation guides, one per kind of record, each ready to read aloud.
  For scenarios: invite a story from a concrete moment, follow it in time, ask what concern sits underneath, ask
  what was contested, read it back. For architectures: invite the pitch, ask which concerns it answers, ask what it
  leaves out, ask who else might hold that concern. In both, show "yes, and" as a second account taken up as a
  companion, and "from this perspective" as every note opening with its group. Include how to return to someone
  who was talked over, and how to record silence. See `strategy.md`, J5a and J5b.
- **J6, coding.** Write the coding guide from what coding needs from capture: raw notes still raw, a group on every
  note, concerns in the speaker's words, gaps and tensions carried across, and a draft label until the group
  confirms. Show "yes, and" as a coder who takes up every awkward line with a placeholder, and "from this perspective" as
  each relation keeping its group and a corrected reading added beside the old one. Where a tool message seems to
  call for a fix that erases a difference, show how to record the difference and mark the gap with a reason
  instead. See `strategy.md`, J6.
