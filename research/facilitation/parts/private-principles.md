# Principles from the supplied drafts

The supplied drafts concern a different setting from facilitation sessions, so they bear on the four topics only
indirectly. Everything here is a paraphrase, unattributed.

## (a) Eliciting stories and proposed designs

Gather accounts from what people are already doing rather than asking them to produce something extra. Whatever a
machine drafts is labelled as such and stays open to its owner, who may correct or withdraw it. Interpretations are
offered to the owner for acceptance, never settled for them.

Proposed designs: silent.

## (b) Keeping multiple perspectives

Each party's account is kept apart and is not blended with others. The party controls what leaves it, and anything
passed outward is passed deliberately and with a record of the terms. Each contribution carries its origin and time.

## (c) Exposing controversy, friction, gaps and dissent

Show where accounts differ, and where a structure depends heavily on one element or has gone unexamined for a long
time. Present these as questions for people to look at, not as findings. Make the quality of people's own checking
visible, so that perfunctory checking can itself be noticed.

## (d) Risks of collapsing disagreement

Condensing many accounts into one tends to favour the commonest view and to hide the structure beneath it, leaving a
group falsely reassured. Pooling accounts also invites misuse of what is revealed.

## What a graph would need to show (capabilities)

- One concern held by several parties, each party's account kept distinct.
- Differing accounts shown together, with the difference left in place.
- Matters left open, not resolved.
- Reliance on a single element, and long-unexamined arrangements.
- Origin, time and status (drafted or confirmed) of each contribution.
- What a contributor agreed to share outward.
- Change over time.
