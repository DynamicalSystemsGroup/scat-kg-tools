title: "Shared data set integrity"
description: "That the KOI data set stays intact and complete for every participant, because what one participant puts in or takes out changes what all of them can use."
---

**The concern.** The data set is the shared knowledge graph the infrastructure builds and maintains. Three narratives call it a club good, and ask for it to stay whole as a thing held in common: nobody can degrade it for themselves alone ([[members-and-the-data-set]], [[the-data-set-and-the-two-layers]], [[forget-incident]]).

**How it is told.** Two scenarios place the concern with the community members, through the participants' shared dependence ([[forget-incident]], [[members-and-the-data-set]]). The architecture narrative states it from the protocol's side and names no holder ([[the-data-set-and-the-two-layers]]). The three use different words (integrity, integrity and completeness, club good) for one concern; the page keeps the single name.

**What gives it weight.** One case on record: a contributor with a large footprint forgot it, and the data set was degraded for others ([[forget-incident]]). The architectural reading holds that an ordinary forget is bounded and expected ([[the-data-set-and-the-two-layers]]); the two readings meet here and the sources do not say how they fit.

**Neighbours.** The forget incident puts it in tension with the right to be forgotten; whether to record that is the coder's call, and a relationship annotation is the place, not an edge. No affordance is linked to it: the architecture narrative says the sources do not say how the infrastructure answers it ([[the-data-set-and-the-two-layers]]).
