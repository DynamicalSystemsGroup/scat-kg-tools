From the point of view of the [[ethnographers|ethnographers]], who work in online forum communities that use KOI. Their method is participatory: they do not observe from outside, the [[community-members|community]] takes part in collecting, and the ethnographers respond to what it says. They call the result building the loop, [[closing-the-loop|closing the loop]]: the study is fed back into the community it studies.

In practice they return their analysis and talk it over with the community. Once they do, the study becomes part of the community's own history, and what it showed members can become part of how the community later sees itself. The notes hold that this separates the work from conventional ethnography, since the loop closes continuously and is not a one-off report.

The notes and data are kept in [[telescope|Telescope]] and [[obsidian|Obsidian]], which the source holds make the records last and be shared ([[durable-shared-records|lasting, shared records]]). Paper notes, private local files or one ethnographer's memory would fade.

The loop can fail to close. Two frictions were named: members may not know about the loop (if people do not reply to the direct message sent to them, the system can stall), and the loop leans on an analysis and synthesis stage that draws on sources and thinking from beyond the field site. The account is that tools can make closing easier and more accessible but cannot close it: someone must have the time, attention and willingness to do the interpretive work. The loop is not automatable.

Thin spots: the notes give the ethnographers' side only. They say nothing of what community members want from the loop, so no concern is coded for them.
