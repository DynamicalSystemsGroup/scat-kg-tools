From the point of view of the [[ethnographers|ethnography working group]], including its principal investigator. They found ethnographic research in online forums hard. The binding problem was [[legitimate-consent-at-forum-scale|consent]]: a large forum is pseudonymous, and the principal investigator had to show a university human-research [[ethics-committees|ethics committee]] that consent could be captured legitimately at that scale. There was also a quiet limit nobody had spoken of, [[attention-limit-on-participation|how many people could take part]], since one ethnographer's attention bounds what can be collected.

The committee was the gate: the research could not have gone ahead without satisfying it. What the notes record of the committee is that it cared whether consent was legitimate when scaled to a forum of this kind.

What cleared the gate was the [[participatory-consent-feature|participatory consent feature]] of [[telescope|Telescope]]. It let consented data be collected by several people, not only the ethnographer, and the community joined in the collecting through emoji mechanisms. The account is that this cut off the older route of solo, observation-only, attention-limited ethnography, and made the overcoming visible. The source positions the loop as the necessary route for consented, participatory ethnography in online forums.

Thin spots: this comes from the ethnographers' side. The committee's own view is not recorded, only what it required and what followed once it was satisfied.
