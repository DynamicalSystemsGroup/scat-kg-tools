Beneficiary organisations are the organisations KOI is built to serve. Their members are the people inside them, whose resources and relationships the infrastructure organises. Separately, community participants are the people in field sites who enact, resist and reshape the loop in everyday practice. Here all of them are clustered as [[community-members|community members]].

The two views read the members differently, and the comparison record lists them among the places where the views diverge. In the architectural view, held here by the [[technical-team|technical team]], members are endpoints of the protocol. In the ethnographic view, held here by the [[ethnographers|ethnographers]], members are the ones whose situated practice makes the loop happen or fails to. Both readings concern [[how-members-figure-in-koi|how members figure in KOI]]; the vault holds both side by side.

What members share is the KOI data set, the shared knowledge graph the infrastructure builds and maintains. The sources call it a club good: its [[shared-data-set-integrity|integrity and completeness]] matter to every participant together, and what one member puts in or takes out affects what the others can use. The concrete case on record is the forget incident at Metagov, told in its own narrative: a contributor with a large footprint forgot it, and the data set was degraded for the others.

Thin spots: the input tells no sequence of events, so scenario is the nearest type. The sources do not list what individual members want from the data set beyond this, and the facilitator has not inferred more. No affordance is coded.
