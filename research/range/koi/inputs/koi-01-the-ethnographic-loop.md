# koi-01-the-ethnographic-loop

Source: ant-rdf `briefs/koi-ethnographic-network.md` (Description; characterizations of the ethnographers); `wiki/Actant-koi--ethnographers.md`

Field note, ethnographic view. The ethnographers work in online forum communities that use KOI. Their method is participatory digital ethnography. They do not observe from outside. The community takes part in collecting, and the ethnographers respond to what the community says. The ethnographers call the result "building the loop": the study is fed back into the community it studies.

In practice the ethnographers return their analysis to the community and talk it over with them. Once that happens, the study is part of the community's own history. It is no longer a record kept apart from the community. What the study showed the members can become part of how the community sees itself later. The ethnographers say this is what separates the work from conventional ethnography: the loop closes again and again, and it is not a one-off report.

The notes and data are kept in Telescope and Obsidian. The ethnographers said these tools make records last and be shared. Paper field notes, private local files, or what stays in one ethnographer's head would fade.

The loop can fail to close. Two friction points were named. First, whether community members even know about the loop. If people do not reply to the direct message sent to them, the whole system can stall. Second, the loop leans on an analysis and synthesis stage that draws on sources and thinking from beyond the field site, so it is not complete in itself.

The ethnographers stressed that tools can make closing the loop easier and more accessible, but cannot close it. Someone must have the time, attention and willingness to do the interpretive work of forming perspectives and putting them to the community. In their words, the loop is not automatable, though it can be given infrastructure and helped along.
