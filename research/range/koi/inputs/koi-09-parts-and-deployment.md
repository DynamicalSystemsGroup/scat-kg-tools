# koi-09-parts-and-deployment

Source: ant-rdf `briefs/koi-architectural-network.md` (participating actants; Description, durability; Mobilization moment); `wiki/Actant-koi--implementation-team.md`; `wiki/Actant-koi--chief-engineer.md`; `wiki/Actant-koi--research-collaborators.md`

Field note, architectural view. The doctrine names the groups around KOI, most with a place in its appendix. Research collaborators supply the requirements and the conceptual framing the protocol is built to satisfy, and they are material to forming the doctrine itself. The implementation team turns the doctrine into running protocol code. Integration teams adapt and deploy KOI at specific field sites. Beneficiary organisations and their members are those KOI serves. The chief engineer is the role accountable for the coherence of the protocol as a whole and speaks for its design integrity.

The doctrine separates the paradigm from its instances. The paradigm is written in the doctrine. The instances are the deployments. The paradigm lasts because instances exist, persist and get used, and they should differ meaningfully while looking alike, so KOI is understood as a pattern language, not a single product.

The doctrine counts the main threat as the pull toward homogenisation. It values requisite variety, and standardisation toward single objectives threatens it, so defending variety takes steady, active advocacy. Decommissioning is not a threat. The doctrine takes a lifecycle view in which an instance arises from a need and is later evolved or retired, and a well-timed, graceful replacement is on-program.

The chief engineer pushes for the paradigm to be understood and adopted, not for any one substrate. The architectural source calls this heterogeneous horizontal scaling.
