# koi-08-the-data-set-and-the-two-layers

Source: ant-rdf `wiki/Actant-koi--data-set.md`; `wiki/Actant-koi--forget-general.md`; `briefs/koi-architectural-network.md` (characterization of Forget invocation (in-program)); `briefs/koi-ethnographic-network.md` (characterization of the subversive forget invocation)

Field note, architectural view. The KOI data set is the shared knowledge graph the infrastructure accumulates and maintains. It is a club good: its integrity and completeness are something all participants depend on collectively.

The architectural account separates two layers. The operative layer is the data and what people do to it. The constitutive layer is the rules and systems that give people the power to act on the data. A routine use of the forget signal changes the data, which the system is designed to allow. It does not change the rules or the systems that afford that power. The architects therefore read it as carrying the signal through faithfully, with the constitutive layer held fixed. Its effect on the data set is expected and bounded, which is what the program of action scripts.

That account says nothing about intent or about how large a footprint someone might have. A forget invocation with a large footprint can hurt other participants even though it works exactly as designed. The architectural source describes itself as silent on the human acts of governance that the ethnographic view foregrounds.

The data set is thus where the two accounts meet. The architectural source treats forget as an affordance working as designed, and the ethnographic source records a case where it degraded the shared graph. The sources do not say how the infrastructure should settle this.
