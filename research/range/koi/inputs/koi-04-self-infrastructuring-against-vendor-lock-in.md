# koi-04-self-infrastructuring-against-vendor-lock-in

Source: ant-rdf `briefs/koi-architectural-network.md` (Translations: Problematization, Interessement, Enrolment moments)

Field note, architectural view. Members of the technical team came to KOI along parallel tracks. They were concerned about tools that do not interoperate, and about vendors controlling not only the data but how it is governed and used, which left data in silos. The source holds that the digital infrastructure had been organised around the vendors' external, fragmented needs, not around the beneficiary organisation and its constituents, the implementers and users. Vendor intermediation was the offence, and KOI was put forward as a de-intermediating, interoperable alternative that returns governance of data to the beneficiary organisation.

What made vendor tools untenable differed by use case. At Metagov the wish was to provide for itself the platforms for its sensemaking, decision-making and identity-formation, and it opposed the data-hoovering of web platforms. For the chief engineer's side the commitment was less about escaping vendors and more about avoiding lock-in and keeping data egress possible. The commitment is to the KOI doctrine, not to any one implementation.

Uptake was uneven. At Metagov it came late, and the first attempts were not really self-infrastructuring. People moved the question of who should serve them elsewhere, and wanted the technical team to be invisible, it-just-works IT, as vendor tools are. This was the "KOI pond" chatbot, which decayed for lack of participation in maintenance and curation and was decommissioned. Later work, "Ask Metagov", set out more clearly who contributes what and where. It looks more successful, but it is new and people outside the team have not yet used its features.

Uptake was strongest on the chief engineer's own technical team, which helps provide, maintain and use its infrastructure, including recording, transcription and search. Applying the doctrine to vendor tools has allowed migrations that would have been very hard otherwise.
