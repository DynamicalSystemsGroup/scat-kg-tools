# koi-06-beneficiary-members-and-the-data-set

Source: ant-rdf `wiki/Actant-koi--beneficiary-org-members.md`; `wiki/Actant-koi--beneficiary-orgs.md`; `wiki/Actant-koi--community-participants.md`; `wiki/Actant-koi--data-set.md`; `briefs/koi-comparison.md`

Field note, from both views. Beneficiary organisations are the organisations KOI is built to serve. Their members are the people inside them, whose resources and relationships the infrastructure organises. Separately, the sources describe community participants as the people in field sites who enact, resist and reshape the loop in their everyday practice.

The two views read the members differently. In the architectural view, members are endpoints of the protocol. The infrastructure organises their resources and relationships. In the ethnographic view, members are the ones whose situated practice makes the loop happen, or fails to make it happen. The comparison record lists the members among the places where the two views diverge.

What the members share is the KOI data set. It is the shared knowledge graph the infrastructure builds up and maintains. The sources call it a club good: its integrity and completeness matter to every participant together. The sources suggest that what one member puts in or takes out affects what the others can use.

For the people involved this has a concrete consequence. The ethnographic source records a case where a contributor had built up a large footprint in the data set, and forgetting it degraded the data set for other participants. The forget incident at Metagov, described elsewhere in these notes, is the case on record.

The sources do not list what individual members want from the data set beyond this. The facilitator has not inferred more.
