# koi-05-integration-teams-at-a-site

Source: ant-rdf `wiki/Actant-koi--integration-teams.md`; `briefs/koi-comparison.md` (table of actants characterized across perspectives; where the frames diverge); `briefs/koi-ethnographic-network.md` (characterization of Integration teams)

Field note, from both views. Integration teams adapt KOI and deploy it at specific field sites. They are the use-case implementers.

From the architectural side, the account is that adapting KOI to a site changes it. An integration team cannot just copy the protocol. The protocol is changed by the act of making it run at that site.

From the ethnographers' side, the account runs the other way. Looking at the field site, they see integration teams carrying KOI's logic into the field faithfully and without changing it. The source holds that there is an asymmetry in this: the integration teams often experience themselves as changing things, as mediating, even though the ethnographers see them as passing things on.

The two groups therefore read the same teams differently, and the comparison record lists the integration teams among the places where the two views diverge. The source does not say which reading is right. It records that the difference depends on who is looking and what they are looking for: the protocol's design, or the ethnographic practice at the field site.

The sources give no sequence of events for any particular site here. They give the two readings of the role.
