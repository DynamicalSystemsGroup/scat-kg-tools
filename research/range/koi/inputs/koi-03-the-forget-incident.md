# koi-03-the-forget-incident

Source: ant-rdf `briefs/koi-ethnographic-network.md` (Description; Enrolment moment; characterizations of the forget invocation); `wiki/Actant-koi--forget-metagov.md`; `wiki/Actant-koi--forget-general.md`

Field note, ethnographic view. In the Metagov field site, a contributor used KOI's forget signal in a way the team experienced as subversive. The team's reading was that the contributor appears to have understood it as speaking up for the right to be forgotten.

By then the contributor had built up a large footprint in the shared data set. When they forgot it, the act punched a hole in the data set. Other participants who relied on it found it degraded. The team described the effect as outsized, far beyond what forget does in ordinary use. The data set is a shared resource that all participants depend on together, so one person's forgetting hurt everyone.

The ethnographers place this within the early, vehement uptake of the consent and emoji processes. They read the contributor as having joined in so as to use the tools' affordances against the configuration. They call this enrolment into resistance. The team saw it as damage to the shared data set. From the contributor's own side it was advocacy for the right to be forgotten, a way of speaking for something, not just running a command.

The ethnographers keep both readings. The incident shows an affordance turned to advocacy that degraded a club good, a matter of intent and consequence that the architecture alone does not show.

Ordinary use of forget is described elsewhere as routine, with a bounded and expected effect on the data set. The incident is set apart because the effect was far larger than the input.
