# koi-02-consent-and-the-ethics-committee-gate

Source: ant-rdf `briefs/koi-ethnographic-network.md` (Translations, Interessement and Problematization moments; characterization of University ethics committees); `wiki/Actant-koi--ethics-committees.md`

Field note, ethnographic view. The ethnography working group found ethnographic research in online forums hard. The binding problem was consent. A large forum is pseudonymous, and the principal investigator (PI) had to show a university human-research ethics committee that consent could be captured legitimately at that scale. Ethnography had a quiet limit that nobody had spoken of: how many people could take part, since one ethnographer's attention bounds what can be collected.

The ethics committee was the gate. The source holds that it is the point the research had to pass through. If the PI had not satisfied it, the research could not have gone ahead. The committee cared about whether consent was legitimate when scaled to a forum of this kind.

What cleared the gate was the participatory mode of Telescope. Its consent feature let consented data be collected by several people, not only the ethnographer. The community itself joined in the collecting through emoji mechanisms. The account is that this cut off the older alternative of attention-limited, solo, observation-only ethnography.

The account is also that the effect was twofold. The participatory mode overcame the limit, and it made the overcoming visible. The source positions the loop as the necessary route for doing consented, participatory ethnography in online forums.

The facilitator notes that this account comes from the ethnographers' side. The committee's own view is not recorded in the source, only what the committee required and what happened once it was satisfied.
