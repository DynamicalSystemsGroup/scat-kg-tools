## Purpose

A project manager at a real field site could not see how to use SCAT (F-8). The docs have a page for each stage but no
summary of the whole flow. They also leave implicit the operational choices that every stage depends on: where the data
is kept, who may use it, and what participants agreed to. WP13 adds two short pages that make both visible. They don't
say which choices are right (log D-069).

## Goals

- **WP13-G1. The flow.** `docs/flow.md`, titled "The flow", placed in the nav right after Home: a simple lifecycle a
  first-time reader can follow, in order:
  1. identify the field site and the participants, and get and document their consent, including what their
     contributions will and will not be used for;
  2. plan the facilitation: structured, semi-structured or unstructured interactions, and whether each is recorded,
     stored or published, as the consents allow;
  3. capture field notes;
  4. code them into the vault;
  5. check the data;
  6. analyse and report.

  Each step names what it depends on from earlier steps, links to the page that teaches it, and stays to a few lines.
  It may use one diagram.
- **WP13-G2. Data, rights and consent.** `docs/data-and-consent.md`, titled "Data, rights and consent". It makes the
  operational details explicit, so that people who choose SCAT are adequately informed:
  - what is collected and in what form (recordings, notes, the vault);
  - where it is stored and who can see it (a private repository is the template's default);
  - consent and how it is documented;
  - what contributions will and will not be used for;
  - whether anything is published, and in what form.

  Each item is named as an activity that a later step depends on, with "for example" illustrations. The page makes no
  normative judgement about what rights, consents or data-use commitments should or should not be offered.
  - **Attribution is a policy choice, shown by example.** The Chatham House rule and personal attribution are two
    examples of a policy an event may adopt (log D-070). The page doesn't say which one to use.
  - **What the tools do is stated as fact, not as advice.** Coded pages name stakeholder groups, never individuals
    (the mission's non-goal). How raw notes are taken is the facilitator's call, within what the participants agreed
    to.
- **WP13-G4. The guides stop asserting one policy.** Today the home page, the planning guide and both facilitation
  guides say every session runs under the Chatham House rule. Reword those passages to say "the participation terms
  agreed for the event" (for example, the Chatham House rule, as the worked example uses), and link to the consent
  page. Change only those passages. What the tools record, groups and not individuals, stays stated as fact.
- **WP13-G3. Joined up.** The home page points first-time readers to the flow. Planning links to the consent page at
  the point where it plans sessions. The terms page (WP12) links to the flow. Each change is at most one line.

## Non-goals

- How these topics were studied, and research citations on them.
- Legal advice, templates for consent forms, or recommended policies.
- Any new field, rule, check or tool. Consent is not recorded in the vault schema.
- Rewriting the existing guides beyond G3's one line each and G4's passages.
