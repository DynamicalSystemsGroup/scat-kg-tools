## Purpose

Give a reader a clear choice of how to code and a walkthrough for each (C14), and rethink the facilitating and planning
pages together so they serve the capture of field notes (C15). Both are quoted in full in `work-packages/candidates.md`.
Words follow `GLOSSARY.md`; every page follows D-139 and D-146 (no prompt residue, no test or work status).

## Goals

- **WP25-G1. Coding approaches, a drill-down.** The navigation's coding section opens on one page that sets out the
  approaches and when each fits, then one walkthrough page per approach:
  - **by hand** (no AI): Obsidian and the `scat` command line;
  - **with an AI IDE** (example: Codex);
  - **with an AI chatbot** (example: Claude);
  - **with a local LLM** (example: Ollama running in Docker, or a better local model if the spike finds one);
  - **leave no trace** (example: inference.coop), for a strict data policy.
  The IDE, chatbot and leave-no-trace flows use different providers, so no tool is favoured.
- **WP25-G2. Screenshots and the fewest clear steps.** Each walkthrough shows a screenshot at each point a person looks
  or chooses; it has as few steps as stay clear, and automation (the launcher, set-up scripts) removes steps only where
  no choice is lost.
- **WP25-G3. Facilitating, narratives first.** The facilitating section opens with **Narratives** (what one is, how
  field notes become one), then Scenarios and Architectures as heuristics for organising and interpreting narratives,
  then what is versus what could be; with attribution of participants as the vault's mode sets it (WP23). All aimed at
  capturing field notes.
- **WP25-G4. Planning by event.** The planning page gives light, high-level guidance by kind of event (a one-off online
  discussion, a recurring discussion, an in-person meetup, a multi-day workshop): how to set the stage for facilitation
  and note taking, who takes field notes, and, with several note takers, how notes are collected and compiled as input
  to coding. Choosing the attribution mode stays in the set-up stage.

## Non-goals

- New tool features; changes to the MCP server beyond what a walkthrough needs to run.
- Bloat: no approach or event kind gets more than one page, and the planning guidance stays high level.
- Choosing or recommending a provider.

## Definition of good

A reader sees at once the ways to code, picks one that fits their data policy and tools, and follows it with pictures;
an organiser plans an event so the field notes come in ready to code.

## Definition of good enough

WP25-R1 to R7 met. Walkthrough runs that need the chief engineer's accounts (Claude, Codex, inference.coop) run in
his sitting, with screenshots taken there.

## Agent's defaults

- *(default)* Screenshots crop out personal details, keys and addresses.
- *(default)* The chatbot and IDE walkthroughs that need his accounts are written first from the providers' own docs,
  then corrected from the run in his sitting.
