# Facilitating scenarios

!!! tip "Your next step"
    Open the session with the participation terms, then ask the first group for a story that starts at a concrete moment.

A scenario is a [narrative](narratives.md) about people doing things in a situation, over time. As a heuristic it tells you what to ask, a story from a concrete moment, and where to put what you hear: under the group it comes from. You run the session, ask the questions and say whose turn it is. A note-taker writes. Nothing is ranked, voted on or settled.

## Before you start

- A plan: groups within your cap, a prompt for each group, a note-taker. See [Planning](planning.md).
- A vault that passes its check. See [Getting started](getting-started.md).

## The stance

- **Yes, and.** Write down what is offered, as offered, and ask a question that adds to it. It means you accept the account, not that you agree with it. If something seems wrong, do not correct or rank it; ask what it adds.
- **From this perspective.** Place every contribution in its group: "from where the operators sit, ...". The group names below are examples; use your own.

Scenarios are behavioural. When a tool comes up, ask what it let people do or stopped them doing. That is an affordance. Do not ask how the tool works.

Take what has happened in one block and what could happen in another: see [Facilitating timeframe](facilitate-timeframe.md).

## The stages

**1. Opening.** Say the participation terms for your vault's [attribution mode](planning.md#choose-the-attribution-mode). Say that today is for stories, and that accounts can differ. The worked example's mode is `disallowed`:

```text
Whatever is said here can be used. Nobody is named; the
notes say which group an account comes from, never who
gave it. Today is for stories about what people do.
Where two stories differ, we write both.
```

The note-taker writes the date, the session and the terms.

**2. Eliciting.** One question at a time, from a concrete moment; wait for each answer.

```text
Tell us about a time when ...
Yes, and what happened next? Who did what?
What were you trying to get done, or protect?
What was contested, or came apart?
```

The note-taker writes what was done in order, each thing a tool let people do or stopped them doing, and each concern in the teller's words on a line of its own.

**3. Taking up.** A second story from the same moment is a second account beside the first, not a rival. Ask the next group: "Yes, and what did that look like from where you sit?"

**4. Placing in perspective.** Name the group each time you turn to someone: "From where the borrowers sit, what did that morning look like?" The note-taker heads each account `From the point of view of GROUP (about 10:20)`, with a name too where the attribution mode says so.

**5. Reading back.** Show the group the note: "Here is what we wrote down from where you sit. What would you add or change?" A correction goes beneath the first wording.

**6. Closing.** Say what happens to the notes: they become a page in a private vault, brought back to each group to add to or change. Ask whether anything should stay in the room; it is left out, with a line saying it was withdrawn.

## Disagreement, silence and interruption

- Two stories that cannot both go well: say so, write both, and ask what each is protecting. Note "Tension: ...", each under its own group.
- The same concern in different words: write each in its own words.
- A group that stays quiet: write "no account offered". That is a gap, not agreement.
- Someone talked over: "Yes, and we haven't yet heard what that looks like from your side."

## Past or future, one at a time

Each scenario is told as what happened or what could happen. [Facilitating timeframe](facilitate-timeframe.md) shows how to tell them apart and keep them in separate blocks.
