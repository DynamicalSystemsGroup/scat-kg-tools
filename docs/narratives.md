# Narratives

!!! tip "Your next step"
    Before the first session, decide how you will head each account in your notes. The attribution mode, set at [planning](planning.md#choose-the-attribution-mode), settles it.

A **narrative** is one story drawn from the field notes, on one page. Everything SCAT draws, the groups, concerns, affordances and technologies and the edges between them, is read out of narratives. Facilitating is the work of getting stories told in a form that can become one.

## Two ways to hear an account

People tell the same ground two ways, and each has a heuristic to organise and interpret what you hear.

| | Scenario | Architecture |
|---|---|---|
| Is | people doing things in a situation, over time | parts assembled into tools that let people do things |
| Tells | groups, concerns, affordances | concerns, affordances, technologies |
| Heard in | [Facilitating scenarios](facilitate-scenarios.md) | [Facilitating architectures](facilitate-architectures.md) |

A third heuristic cuts across both: whether an account is of what was or of what could be. See [Facilitating timeframe](facilitate-timeframe.md).

None of the three is a form to fill in. They tell you what to ask next and what to jot down, so the notes come out in a shape the coder can use. A narrative is sometimes neither a scenario nor an architecture; the notes are still taken the same way.

## What good field notes hold

- **The group.** Every story is headed by the group it comes from, with a rough time.
- **The account as offered.** In the speaker's words, in the order told. Concerns get a line each, quoted.
- **Differences, kept.** Two accounts of one moment are written side by side. A group that said nothing is written down as "no account offered".
- **Withdrawals.** Anything a group asks to leave out is left out, with a line saying so.

Notes are written during the session and are not tidied afterwards. Meaning is added later, in coding.

## Who is named

The vault's attribution mode decides what heads an account, and the note-taker follows it:

| Mode | The note-taker writes |
|---|---|
| `disallowed` | The group only. Never a name. |
| `optional` | The group, and a name or handle only for a speaker who asked for one. |
| `required` | The group, and the name or handle of each speaker. |

What to say aloud for each mode is in [Planning](planning.md#choose-the-attribution-mode), and [Data, rights and consent](data-and-consent.md#attribution-mode) says what the vault then holds. The worked example runs in `disallowed`.

## From notes to a narrative

One story told, or one design pitched, becomes one narrative page, with what every group that spoke to it said, each under its own group. A different story is a different page.

```text
Chatham House: groups, not individuals.

From the point of view of Operators (about 10:20)
- Saturday, desk opens at 9. Three people ask for the same drill.
- Concern: "we cannot lend a tool we have not checked."

From the point of view of Borrowers (about 10:35)
- We queued, and nobody said how long.
- Concern: "I need it for the afternoon, not next week."

Tension: Operators want a check before a tool leaves. Borrowers want it in hand now.

Trustees and funders: no account offered.
```

With several note-takers, each writes in the same shape, and the notes are gathered into one set before coding. [Planning](planning.md) says who takes them and how they are gathered.

The coder takes it from here: [Coding](coding.md) makes the page, sets its `kind` and `timeframe`, links the nodes and records the edges.
