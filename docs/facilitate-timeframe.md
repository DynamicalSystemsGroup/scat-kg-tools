# Facilitating timeframe

!!! tip "Your next step"
    Decide which block you are in, what happened or what could happen, and ask the questions for that block.

Every [narrative](narratives.md) is an account of what was, of what could be, or of both. That is its timeframe, and it is recorded on the page as `past`, `future` or `mixed`. As a heuristic it tells you what to ask and what to note, in a scenario or an architecture alike.

The mark is about whether what is told is real, not about the verb. Anything real that can be narrated is past, including a habit and something that exists now. Anything done with intention but not yet realized is future. The present folds into one of the two.

| | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|
| Scenarios | what people did | what people could do or will do |
| Architectures | a system that exists, or once did | a system that is proposed |

## One at a time

People mix the two freely. Lead them to one at a time: stories of what happened in one block, and what could happen in another. If the other time comes up early, jot it on a scrap or the board, carry it to that block, and return to it then.

## What to ask in each

| Block | Ask | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Past | "Tell us about a time when ..." "What happened next?" | What was done, in order, and what it let people do or stopped them doing. |
| Future | "What could happen if ...?" "What would you want it to do?" | What is hoped for, and the concern behind it, marked as not yet real. |

For a trial under way, the trial itself is past, and what it is meant to achieve is future. Ask for each separately.

## Telling them apart

| Phrase | Mark | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "We usually run out of drills by noon." | past | Usual means it has happened. |
| "People can see who has what on the sheet." | past | Present tense, but it exists. |
| "We tried a spreadsheet once and dropped it." | past | It happened, though it is no longer in use. |
| "What would happen if we lent drills overnight?" | future | A counterfactual is not yet real. |
| "We are moving to a new sheet this month." | future | Intended, not yet realized. |
| "The new app will replace the sheet." | future | Proposed. |
| "The sheet stays, and an app will sit on top of it." | mixed | Part exists, part is proposed, and they hang together. |

`mixed` is for an account that blurs the two and cannot be cleanly separated, for example "it never worked, so we hope the new one will". It is the honest record, not a failure. For an architecture it is the normal mark when one account describes what exists and what is proposed.
