# Facilitating architectures

!!! tip "Your next step"
    Open the session with the participation terms, then ask the first proposer how the parts fit together and what the whole lets people do.

An architecture is a [narrative](narratives.md) about how parts are put together into tools that let people do things. As a heuristic it tells you what to ask, how the parts fit and what worry each design answers, and where to put what you hear: under the group that proposes it. Each design is heard in full, one proposer at a time, and none is set against another. A note-taker writes. Nothing is ranked or voted on.

## Before you start

- A plan: groups within your cap, a note-taker, and the people who will pitch. See [Planning](planning.md).
- A vault that passes its check. See [Getting started](getting-started.md).
- Scenario sessions kept apart, in a separate session or a clearly separate half. See [Facilitating scenarios](facilitate-scenarios.md).

## The stance

- **Yes, and.** Write down what is pitched, as offered, and ask a question that adds to it. It means you accept the design, not that you agree with it.
- **From this perspective.** Tie each pitch to the group that proposes it: "from where the operators sit, ...". It is never the standard way.

Architectures are structural: parts, wholes and the concerns they answer. People are largely set aside, since a design cannot say who will adopt it. Technologies and parts are named freely. Anyone with a design to pitch is a technologist for the session; that is a role for the session, not a label for the notes.

Take what exists in one block and what is proposed in another: see [Facilitating timeframe](facilitate-timeframe.md).

## The stages

**1. Opening.** Say the participation terms for your vault's [attribution mode](planning.md#choose-the-attribution-mode). Say that today is for designs and the concerns they answer, and that several designs can answer one concern. The worked example's mode is `disallowed`:

```text
Whatever is said here can be used. Nobody is named; the
notes say which group a pitch comes from, never who gave
it. Today is for how things are put together, and what
worry each one is for.
```

The note-taker writes the date, the session and the terms.

**2. Eliciting.** One question at a time; wait for each answer.

```text
How do the parts fit together, and what does the whole let people do?
What worry or aim is this for?
Yes, and is there a concern this was not aimed at?
Yes, and who else might hold that concern?
```

The note-taker writes each part and how it joins the others, each concern in the proposer's words on a line of its own, each gap as "not addressed: ..." (a fact about the design, without blame), and who else might hold the concern as an open question. If someone starts on the story behind the design, say "Yes, and thank you. We will note that story for the scenario session," and write it as a separate account.

**3. Taking up.** A second design for the same concern is written beside the first, not against it: "Yes, that answers it, and here is another way to answer it?"

**4. Placing in perspective.** "From where the operators sit, how do the parts fit?" The note-taker heads each pitch `From the point of view of GROUP (about 14:05)`, with a name too where the attribution mode says so, and writes concerns in the words of the group that voiced them. Near-identical concerns in different words are both kept.

**5. Capturing.** Write what was offered before anyone reads meaning into it. A proposer's revision goes beneath the first wording.

**6. Closing.** Say what happens to the notes: they become a page in a private vault, brought back to each group to add to or change. Ask whether anything should stay in the room; it is left out, with a line saying it was withdrawn.

## Disagreement, silence and interruption

- Two designs that cannot sit together: write both, and ask what each is protecting. Note "Tension: ...", each under its own group.
- A group that stays quiet: write "no account offered". That is a gap, not agreement.
- Someone talked over: "Yes, and we haven't yet heard what that looks like from your side."

## What the notes look like

```text
Chatham House: groups, not individuals.

From the point of view of Operators (about 14:05)
Pitch: a booking sheet, a shelf label for each tool, and a weekly check by two volunteers.
- Parts: the sheet records who has what; the label shows the tool's check date.
- Concern: "we cannot lend a tool we have not checked."
- Not addressed: tools that go missing between checks.
- Who else might hold this concern: open.

Borrowers: no account offered.
```

One pitch is one narrative page. Do not link other pages or say who will use a design; [Coding](coding.md) adds the links and the edges.
